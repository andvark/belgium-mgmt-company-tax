# Comparison B: the three arrangements under each draft proposal (scenario assumptions)

> **RULES SUPPLIED, NOT CONFIRMED; PROPOSAL PARAMETERS ARE SCENARIO ASSUMPTIONS, NOT THE DRAFT.**
>
> **This is not an analysis of the government's proposal.** The draft text was not available. P1 (EUR 100,000 band, no employees) is as reported; P2 (payroll multiple 2x/3x) and P3 (client threshold 70-100%, lookback 3/5 years) come from second-hand excerpts and are run as grids of scenario assumptions; the mechanics below (what counts as payroll, how a cap is applied, how the client test is evaluated, what a hire costs) are **my modelling choices**, listed in section 1. The rules underneath are those the owner's checklist review of 15 November 2026 reported confirmed (status counts of rules used: `{'owner_confirmed': 30, 'assumed': 19, 'scenario': 5, 'open': 4, 'supplied': 1}`; this analysis has not opened the primary pages itself) - what remains is in [`hand_check_list.md`](hand_check_list.md). No entrepreneur count, behavioural response in entry/exit, or budget figure is computed here: that is comparison C.

Generated by `scripts/make_report_b.py` from `outputs/comparison_B/` (code commit `3ba8db0`; seed 20261115; 300 paired replicates, identical revenue and client-share paths in every scenario; 25 scenarios, 101 distinct runs x 3 household types = 303 simulations (one-client owners are run once per family and copied: see the script); max ledger residual 1.3e-09). Mature company (already in its 5th taxable period in 2026), horizon 2026-2040, single owner unless stated.

## 1. Scenario definitions (all assumptions)

| proposal | start | as modelled | open / unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | 2027 (also 2028) | the 20% rate on the first EUR 100,000 requires the company to have a paid employee. An owner may hire at an annual employer cost c (EUR 5,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 / 20,000; c = 1 is a diagnostic 'free hire'); the cost is treated as an expense leaving the system (no tax on it counted) | what counts as an employee (hours, months, relatives, flexi-jobs); minimum cost c |
| **P2** | 2028 (also 2027) | in each year the reduced regimes (VVPRbis dividends, liquidation-reserve allocations and dividends) are limited to payroll / m, payroll = director pay + hired employee's gross wage in the same year; the excess dividend is taxed at 30%, the excess allocation stays in the free reserve; the cap applies to old reserves too. m = 2 and 3 | payroll period, treatment of the excess, old reserves, liquidation payouts |
| **P3** | 2028 | profit of a year in which the owner's largest-client share was >= threshold in every year of the lookback window (current year included) goes to an ordinary reserve (30% dividend or liquidation) and cannot enter the liquidation reserve; amounts of earlier years keep their regime. Threshold 70/80/90/100%, lookback 3/5 years | client definition, intermediaries and related clients, number of years, treatment of existing reserves |
| **combined** | P1 2027, P2 and P3 2028 | all of the above together; owners may also raise their pay (fixed-pay strategies 60k/80k/100k) and hire | |

Client concentration is **not data**: owners are simulated with a mean largest-client share of 100% (exactly one client: segment S1), 95, 90, 80, 70 or 50% and an assumed year-to-year fluctuation (sd 8 points, persistence 0.5, 5 years of history). Owners below the threshold are in S0 but untouched by P3. The share of S0 owners at each concentration is unknown (open input), so results are reported by concentration, not as an average.

Two views of an owner's response are shown: **static** = keep the strategy that was best under current law and do not hire (fixed behaviour); **re-optimised** = the best of 28 strategies (including higher pay levels) and, for P1, the best hire choice, i.e. the owner adapts the tax planning but still exists. Neither includes entry or exit of companies.

Reading: P1 takes the 20% rate away from every company that has it today, including owners who pay themselves less than EUR 51,000 but enough to cover the taxable income left after that pay (the cheapest qualifying salary), so the static loss is EUR 1,184-4,100 a year for an owner who keeps that strategy. Owners who pay themselves only what the household needs ('minimum pay') mostly gave up the 20% rate already and are hardly affected. The cheapest response is therefore to switch to minimum pay (re-optimised loss EUR 949 against EUR 3,407 static at EUR 160,000), not to hire: a hire pays for itself only if it costs less than about EUR 1.5-2.1 thousand a year, far below any realistic employee. The budget effect of P1 therefore depends on how many owners keep the 20% rate - the distribution of director pay in S0 is an open input (not available).

**Deferral.** An owner who keeps the profit in the liquidation reserve pays the reserve levy (10% of the credited amount, 9.1% of the profit) every year under current law. When P2 or P3 keeps part of that profit out of the reserve, the levy is not paid and the extra tax (30% on liquidation) arrives only when the company is wound up - here in 2040, an artefact of the 15-year horizon. So for owners on this route the static effect on revenue in 2028-2039 is **negative** and positive only at the end; for owners who pay annual dividends the effect is positive at once. The sign and size of the early-year revenue therefore depend on the mix of owner types, which is not known.

## 10. What this does and does not show

- It shows, **under rules that the owner's review reported confirmed (not opened by this analysis) and my reading of second-hand proposal descriptions**, how each proposal changes the tax advantage of a one-person BV for a tax-minimising owner, by revenue level and owner type, and where the avoidance margins are (hire, higher pay, diversifying clients - the last is not modelled).
- It does **not** show the number of entrepreneurs, the budget, or the wealth distribution, and does not model entry/exit, relocation, incidence or the choice of arrangement (WP3-4).
- Owner types are archetypes: the distribution of director pay, retained profits and client concentration across S0 and S1 is **not known**; the budget result depends on it more than on any proposal parameter.
- The loss columns use the 15-year terminal wealth including a liquidation in 2040; per-year figures are averages over 2026-2040 (so proposals starting in 2027/2028 are diluted by pre-start years).
- A P3 owner could dip below the threshold by adding a minor client (cost unknown): not modelled; it would reduce the P3 effect.
